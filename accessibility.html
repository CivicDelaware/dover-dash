Accessibility

Last updated: August 27, 2026

Our commitment

Dover Dash is meant to be usable by everyone, regardless of how they access the web. We aim to follow standard web accessibility practices so the site works well with the assistive technologies people already use, whether that's a screen reader, an alternative input device, or built-in accessibility features on a phone or computer.

What that looks like in practice

We build with real, standard HTML elements rather than custom controls that behave unpredictably. Buttons are actual buttons, links are actual links, and pages don't rely on hover-only interactions or unusual scroll behavior. Buttons are also sized and spaced with room for error, not packed tightly together, since precise clicking or tapping can be difficult for people with tremor or other motor conditions.

This is what allows the site to work with screen readers (like JAWS, NVDA, or VoiceOver), switch access, eye-tracking and gaze control, cursor-smoothing tools, voice control, and other alternative input methods, since these tools generally work by controlling standard clicks, taps, and keyboard input rather than requiring anything site-specific.

An ongoing effort

Accessibility isn't a one-time checklist, and we don't claim the site is perfect. As Dover Dash grows and adds new features, we aim to keep accessibility in mind rather than treating it as an afterthought.

Let us know if something doesn't work

If you use assistive technology and run into something on Dover Dash that doesn't work the way it should, we want to know. Reach out at info@civicdelaware.com and describe what happened, including the tool or device you were using. We'll do our best to fix it.
